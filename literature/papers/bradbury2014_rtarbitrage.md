## 1. Research question
Given real-time price volatility in U.S. markets, what is the profit-maximising energy-to-power sizing of different storage technologies and can they reach an attractive internal rate of return (IRR) from arbitrage alone?

## 2. Setting & assumptions
(from abstract) Price-taker; 2008 real-time prices in seven U.S. markets; linear optimisation of storage capacity and operation; 14 technologies characterised by round-trip efficiency, self-discharge and power/energy costs. Foresight assumption, resolution and degradation treatment not verified (full text unread).

## 3. Constraints that drove the model choice
(from abstract) Not stated in abstract; LP chosen for co-optimising size and dispatch.

## 4. Model
(from abstract) Linear optimisation maximising arbitrage profit net of annualised capital cost over storage power/energy capacity and hourly dispatch. Exact formulation not read.

## 5. Data & processing
(from abstract) 2008 real-time prices, seven U.S. RTO/ISO markets.

## 6. Justification (why the authors argue the approach is valid)
Not assessable from abstract.

## 7. Key results
(from abstract)
- Profit-maximising ESS size is determined primarily by technology characteristics (round-trip efficiency, self-discharge), **not** by market price volatility.
- Optimal storage durations: pumped hydro and CAES ≈ 7–8 h; most other systems 1–4 h.
- Deployment will remain limited until ESSs achieve an attractive IRR (arbitrage-only IRRs reported as insufficient for most technologies).

## 8. Limitations (stated + your critical reading)
Critical (from abstract): single year (2008, high gas prices); arbitrage only; price-taker. Full stated limitations not read.

## 9. Relevance to my study
Cross-market comparison template (same model, many markets) — the key finding that sizing is technology- rather than market-driven is a hypothesis to retest with modern battery costs and multi-product rules.
