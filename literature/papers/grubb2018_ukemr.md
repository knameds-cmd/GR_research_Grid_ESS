## 1. Research question
What has GB's 2013 Electricity Market Reform achieved and what lessons emerge for market design in the energy transition? (from abstract)

## 2. Setting & assumptions
Ex-post evaluation of four EMR instruments (Carbon Price Floor, CfDs for low-carbon generation, Capacity Market auctions, Emissions Performance Standard) in GB's energy-only wholesale market. (from abstract)

## 3. Constraints that drove the model choice
Policy evaluation of a package of simultaneous instruments - attribution is narrative/descriptive rather than model-based. (interpretation)

## 4. Model
None (descriptive/analytical evaluation).

## 5. Data & processing
GB outturn data on generation mix, emissions, auction results (details not read).

## 6. Justification
Comparison of outcomes to pre-reform expectations/predictions (auction prices "far lower than predicted"). (from abstract)

## 7. Key results (from abstract)
- Competitive auctions for firm capacity and renewables cleared at prices far below predictions and admitted unexpected new technologies (storage/small gas engines in the Capacity Market are the canonical example - not confirmed from text).
- Carbon price floor displaced coal: share fell from 46% (1995) to 7% (2017), roughly halving power-sector CO2.
- Renewables rose from < 4% (2008) to 22% (2017), projected 30%+ by 2020.
- Outstanding challenges: transmission pricing and locational signals; EMR "is not the end of the story".

## 8. Limitations (stated + critical reading)
Simultaneous instruments make causal attribution to any single rule impossible; written before the 2018 Capacity Market state-aid suspension and before battery de-rating reforms (2017-18) fully played out.

## 9. Relevance to my study
Institutional backdrop for the GB part of the study: explains why GB batteries first earned from Capacity Market and frequency-response tenders rather than energy arbitrage. Illustrates the attribution problem with policy packages - motivates simulation-based counterfactuals over before/after comparison.
