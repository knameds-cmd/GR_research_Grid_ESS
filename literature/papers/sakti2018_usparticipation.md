## 1. Research question
How do current federal, ISO/RTO and state regulations and market rules govern advanced (mainly electrochemical) storage participation in US wholesale markets, and what reforms - including whether FERC Order 841 suffices - are needed for efficient storage integration? (from abstract)

## 2. Setting & assumptions
Regulatory review, no quantitative model. Scope: participation models and rules across restructured US markets at all timescales (energy, ancillary services, capacity). (from abstract)

## 3. Constraints that drove the model choice
Fragmented, jurisdiction-specific rules make a comparative institutional review (rather than a single-market model) the appropriate method. (from abstract, interpretation)

## 4. Model
None (qualitative comparative review of participation models across ISOs/RTOs).

## 5. Data & processing
Regulatory documents (FERC orders, ISO/RTO tariffs, state policies) as of ~2018. Details not read.

## 6. Justification
Comprehensive coverage across federal/ISO/state levels. (from abstract)

## 7. Key results
- Participation models for storage across US restructured markets are fragmented. (from abstract)
- Market reforms are needed "at all timescales" to integrate storage efficiently; Order 841 is assessed as a framework for participation. (from abstract)
- Specific ISO-by-ISO findings (minimum size, SoC management, duration requirements for capacity, metering/charging settlement) NOT extracted - full text not read.

## 8. Limitations (stated + critical reading)
Snapshot at 2018 (pre-implementation of Order 841 compliance filings, 2019-2020); review rather than quantitative evaluation; cannot attribute outcomes to rules.

## 9. Relevance to my study
Reference catalogue of the US design levers (participation model, SoC management, capacity duration rules, ancillary-service eligibility) to map onto switchable "rule settings" in a simulator; contrast with EU/GB rules. Should be read in full before use as a rule taxonomy.
