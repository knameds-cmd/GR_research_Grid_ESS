## 1. Research question
Does a profit-maximising merchant storage operator invest in a socially optimal amount of storage in an imperfectly competitive market, and can a simple market-design instrument align its investment with the welfare optimum? (from abstract)

## 2. Setting & assumptions
- Bilevel: upper level = storage capacity investment; lower level = generation and storage operation in an imperfectly competitive market. (from abstract)
- Off-peak/on-peak periods (implied by "ramping between off- and on-peak periods"). Other assumptions (demand form, number of firms, efficiency) not read.

## 3. Constraints that drove the model choice
Investment decision anticipates its effect on equilibrium prices and on generators' strategic behaviour -> Stackelberg/bilevel structure. (from abstract)

## 4. Model
Upper level: merchant (or welfare-maximising) storage firm chooses capacity. Lower level: Cournot-type generation and storage operation equilibrium. Counterfactual instrument: a charge on generation ramping between off- and on-peak periods. Exact formulation not read.

## 5. Data & processing
Not read (analytical + illustrative numerics presumed).

## 6. Justification
Analytical results (comparative statics in degree of generation market power). (from abstract)

## 7. Key results (from abstract)
- High (low) generation market power -> merchant invests less (more) storage than a welfare maximiser.
- Merchant investment can produce net welfare losses vs no storage; losses are guaranteed if the generation sector is sufficiently competitive.
- A ramping charge between off- and on-peak periods induces the merchant to invest the welfare-maximising capacity and can raise welfare above the welfare-maximising-operator case.

## 8. Limitations (stated + critical reading)
Two-period stylisation; energy-only; single merchant; ramping-charge instrument is a theoretical device with no direct real-world counterpart. Full text not read.

## 9. Relevance to my study
Adds a constructive design remedy (price instrument that corrects storage investment) to the ownership/market-power line; illustrates the "design instrument as counterfactual" pattern: welfare with instrument vs without vs planner. Analogue for GB: products that pay for ramping/flexibility (e.g., dynamic response services) alter storage investment incentives.
